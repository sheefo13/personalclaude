# 30 Things You'll Only Understand If You Grew Up Arab in the Diaspora

Whether you grew up in Dearborn, London, Sydney, or Toronto — if your parents were Arab, certain experiences are *universal*. Here are 30 things every Arab diaspora kid knows by heart.

1. **"We're leaving in 5 minutes"** means at least 45 minutes of goodbye rituals at the door.

2. Your mom telling the guests you're a doctor/engineer **before you're a doctor/engineer**.

3. The **mandatory "I'm full"** before accepting more food. (You're not full. You'll never be full.)

4. Being called by **three names** when you're in trouble — your name, your dad's name, and your grandfather's name.

5. **"Habibi/habibti"** being used in both the most loving and most threatening ways within seconds of each other.

6. Every family gathering eventually becoming about **who's getting married** next.

7. The **plastic-covered couches** in the formal living room that no one was allowed to sit on.

8. Your parents' **volume setting** being either silent or extremely loud, with no in between.

9. The guest bathroom that looked like a **museum exhibit** — towels you couldn't use.

10. **Knocking the evil eye** (ayn) every time someone complimented something.

11. Food being the answer to **every emotional situation** — sad? Eat. Happy? Eat. Heartbroken? Your mom made you maqluba.

12. The pressure to speak **perfect Arabic** while your Arabic slowly became a dialect-English hybrid.

13. **"What will people say?"** being the basis of at least 40% of all family decisions.

14. Your home smelling like **oud and cardamom** and being completely unbothered by it.

15. Non-Arab friends being absolutely **overwhelmed** by the amount of food at your house.

16. The specific anxiety of bringing a non-Arab friend home for the **first time**.

17. Arab parents **bragging about you** to everyone except you — to your face it's all criticism.

18. **Sha'er (rice)** being a side dish to every main dish including other rice dishes.

19. The **"yalla yalla"** that means everything from "hurry up" to "okay okay I hear you."

20. **Long-distance calls to relatives** that lasted hours and made no financial sense.

21. Your parents knowing the **entire extended family tree** of every Arab family in your city.

22. The unspoken rule that **guests eat first, family eats whatever's left**.

23. **Sitto/Teta/Jiddo** slipping you money when your parents weren't looking.

24. Arabic music at a **volume that was non-negotiable**.

25. The **specific shame** of your Arab mom yelling your name across a parking lot.

26. Growing up bilingual but your **two languages mixing** into one beautiful mess.

27. **Hospitality as identity** — no one leaves your house without eating something.

28. Being told to **marry Arab** but also to be successful in the Western world — no notes on how to do both.

29. The **group family photo** that required 45 minutes of negotiation to organize.

30. Despite everything — feeling **deeply, completely Arab**, wherever in the world you are.
